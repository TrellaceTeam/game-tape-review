# Run sheet

The template for `run-sheet.md` and the rules for writing it. The leader runs game tape from this file and copies blocks from it into a meeting agenda, so each block stands on its own.

## Writing rules

- **Quotes.** Quote the rep and the buyer exactly as `"[HH:MM:SS] exact words"`, copied from one transcript line with its own punctuation. Cut words with "..." where you need to, but never cut a "not", a number or a name. Inside a quote, use single quotes. The check in step 7 finds every quote in this form.
- **Links.** Label each link with the clip's times in m:ss, or h:mm:ss past an hour, such as `[4:40 to 6:49](link)`. The link opens at the first time. When the recorder has no start-time links, write `[Open at 4:40](plain link)` and say "scrub to 4:40".
- **Why this step matters.** The step's job in one or two sentences: what the rep is trying to get done and why it moves the deal.
- **Set it up.** What the room needs to follow the clip, since it hasn't seen the rest of the call: who the buyer is, by first name and role, and anything said earlier that the clip refers to.
- **What's good.** The specific moves, each with its quote. Name the moves that build trust, such as booking the next meeting while still on the call, getting the buyer to open their calendar, framing the next step as good for the buyer, or offering a direct phone number.
- **The near-miss.** The job left undone, and the words that would have done it. Leave out nitpicks about where a question sat in the call, and technique labels, unless the match setting is exact lines.
- **What came before.** For a pause clip, the answer key: what the buyer said earlier, each with its time, so the leader can share it after the room answers.
- **Ask the room.** Two or three questions the room can answer from the clip and the setup line. Good shapes: "Think of your last demo. What did the buyer tell you early on that you could bring back like this?", "What would you say next?" and "How would you ask that without sounding pushy?" Leave out quiz questions about parts of the call the room didn't see.
- **Kind.** Credit what went well before the near-miss. Write about the move, never the person.
- **Everything else.** Follow [writing.md](writing.md). Its rules cover your words, never the quotes.

## Template

Leave out any block a clip doesn't need, such as part 2 or the answer key.

```markdown
# Game tape: {meeting name}, {date}

Play {rep 1}, then {rep 2}. {One sentence on the thread the clips follow through a call.} {Minutes} minutes of video in all. Plan on {total} minutes with the questions.

## Open with this

"We're working on our {process name, such as first-call process} together. Here's the doc: [{playbook name}]({playbook link}). Each of you has two short clips. One shows a step done well. In the other we'll pause and ask: what would you say here? The person on the call speaks first. Then we each add one thing that worked and one thing to try. By the end we'll pick one thing we'll all try on our next calls."

## Your picks at a glance

The recommended clip for each slot is A. B and C are ready to swap in. Their details are under Swaps.
```
