# Built-in first-call playbook

Use this when the team has no playbook of its own. It covers a first call with a B2B buyer, in the order most calls run. Each step has its job, what a good rep says, and the near-miss that game tape catches most often. Words in [brackets] stand for the buyer's own details.

## 1. Prep

- **Job.** Walk in knowing who the buyer is, what their company does and why they took the call, so the first minute is about them.
- **Sounds like.** "I saw you opened [a third plant] this year. Congratulations."
- **Near-miss.** A generic opener, or questions the buyer's own website answers.

## 2. Open and set the agenda

- **Job.** Agree on what the call is for and how long it runs, and ask what the buyer wants to cover, so they help steer.
- **Sounds like.** "Here's what I'd like to do with our 30 minutes. Does that work? Anything you want to make sure we cover?"
- **Near-miss.** Diving straight into a demo, or an agenda the buyer never agreed to.

## 3. Discovery

- **Job.** Learn the problem in the buyer's words, with an example, what it costs them, why it matters now and what they've tried before.
- **Sounds like.** "When did that last happen?" "Have you put a number on what it cost?" "Why now, and not six months ago?"
- **Near-miss.** Taking the first answer and moving on. The buyer mentions a past attempt, a rival product or a deadline, and the rep passes it by.

## 4. Say it back

- **Job.** Read back what you heard, in the buyer's words, and get a yes. It shows you listened, and it sets up everything after it.
- **Sounds like.** "Let me play back what I heard. [Their problem, its cost, their date]. Did I get that right?"
- **Near-miss.** A summary in the rep's words, or no summary at all.

## 5. Tell the story

- **Job.** Give the buyer a reason to believe before the demo: why the company exists and a customer like them, tied to what they said.
- **Sounds like.** "A [company like yours] had the same thing. You said [their words]. Here's what changed for them. Does that ring true for you?"
- **Near-miss.** The same story for every buyer, or no check-in at the end.

## 6. Show it

- **Job.** Show only what solves what the buyer described, in their words. When the buyer starts picturing it at their own company, stay with them.
- **Sounds like.** "You said [their words]. This is what that looks like." Then, when they picture it: "Walk me through what [their person] would do with this."
- **Near-miss.** A feature tour, or racing past the moment the buyer leans in.

## 7. Doubts and fit

- **Job.** Bring out what could stop the deal, and answer honestly, including "we don't do that yet."
- **Sounds like.** "What would make this not work for you?" "What happened with the tool you tried last spring?"
- **Near-miss.** A worry the buyer raised that never gets an answer, or a promise of a feature that isn't built.

## 8. How they decide

- **Job.** Learn who decides, what matters to each of them and the steps to a yes, and get the person who signs onto the next call.
- **Sounds like.** "How do decisions like this get made?" "What will matter most to [the person who signs]?" "Could they join us next time?"
- **Near-miss.** A name and nothing more. The rep learns who signs but not what they care about, and never invites them.

## 9. Price

- **Job.** Give a fair range when asked, tied to the cost the buyer named, without discounting on a first call.
- **Sounds like.** "Teams like yours usually invest [range]. You said [the problem] cost you [their number]."
- **Near-miss.** Price before the buyer has seen any value, or a discount nobody asked for.

## 10. Book the next step

- **Job.** End with the next meeting on the calendar, with a date, a time and the right people, framed as good for the buyer.
- **Sounds like.** "Can we put 30 minutes on the calendar now? I have [two times]. I'll send the invite while we're on." Then: "That'll have my cell in it too."
- **Near-miss.** "I'll send some info, let me know." A hot call ends with nothing booked.

## Great listening, in any step

A buyer names a struggle in their own words, or starts picturing the product at their own company, and the rep goes with it. Look for these moments in every step. They make the best good clips, and when the rep passes one by, it makes the best pause clip.
