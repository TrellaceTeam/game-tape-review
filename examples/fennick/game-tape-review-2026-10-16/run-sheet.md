## The clips to play

### 1. Priya, good: Harrow Creek (Oct 6, first call)

**Why this step matters.** The person who signs rarely sits on the first call. If they hear the case from someone on their own team instead of from us, they're far more likely to believe it. Asking for them, and booking a date, is the step that moves the deal.

**Set it up.** Ruth runs maintenance at Harrow Creek Castings, a foundry with two plants. Ruth told Priya that Walt, the plant manager, has to believe the crew will use it, and that Sandra, the GM, signs. Sandra sets next year's budget the week before Thanksgiving. Ruth hasn't told Walt she's talking to anyone.

**Play: [18:04 to 20:55](https://recorder.example/share/c2Hd6Harrow1006?t=1084).** Stop after Priya says the invite is sent.

**What's good.**
- Asks for both people by name and frames it as good for Ruth: "[00:18:04] Would Walt and Sandra be willing to join the next conversation? Honestly, I think it would go better than me trying to reach them through you."
- Answers Ruth's worry about Sandra's time, and gives Walt the lead: "[00:18:29] Thirty minutes. And I'd want Walt doing most of the talking, not me. What would Walt need to see in that half hour to believe his crew would use it?"
- Books it while both are on the call, with two times and a reason: "[00:19:29] Great. Then let's get it on the calendar while we're both here, so it doesn't turn into a week of email. I have Tuesday the 13th at 10 or Wednesday the 14th at 2, Eastern. And the reason I'd push for it is so Sandra hears it from Walt, not from a vendor."
- Sends the invite to all three and checks it landed: "[00:20:39] Got it. I'm sending it now to all three of you, Wednesday the 14th, 2 to 2:30 Eastern. Okay, it's sent. Did it come through on your end?"

**The near-miss.** None. Just after the clip, Priya gives Ruth a cell number and Ruth says Ruth will walk over and tell Walt in person. The clip stops before the number.

**Ask the room.**
1. Think of your last first call. Who signs, and what did you say about getting them on the next one?
2. Priya gave a reason that was good for the buyer: "Sandra hears it from Walt, not from a vendor." What's that reason on your deals?

### 2. Priya, pause: Sable Point (Oct 13, first call)

**Why this step matters.** When a buyer says "I'll run it by my boss," that's the moment to learn who the boss is and ask to put them on the next call. If it passes, the buyer carries our case alone and the next step becomes an email.

**Set it up.** Greg runs maintenance at Sable Point Packaging, a one-plant corrugated box maker. Priya has just finished a long demo and answered Greg's price question. The clip starts after the price talk.

**Play part 1: [15:22 to 15:54](https://recorder.example/share/q5Ws1Sable1013?t=922).** Pause right after Greg says "[00:15:43] I like it. I really do. I'll need to run it by my boss. He'll want to see numbers."

**Ask the room while it's paused.**
1. What would you say next?

**What came before.** The answer key, to share after the room answers.
- 12:02. "[00:12:02] Does that break it out by machine? My plant manager, Neil Ostrowski, keeps asking me why the corrugator keeps going down."
- 12:28. "[00:12:28] That'd be nice. Something to show him besides my spreadsheet. He's not a spreadsheet guy."
- 9:31. "[00:09:31] Sorry, one thing before you move on. I've got budget left this year if we move before December."
- 7:41. "[00:07:41] This is exactly what we need. Honestly, this would fix half my week."

**Play part 2: [15:54 to 16:40](https://recorder.example/share/q5Ws1Sable1013?t=954).**

**What's good.**
- Priya takes the "numbers" ask seriously and promises Greg something to hand the boss: "[00:16:10] I'll send our overview deck, a one-pager on pricing, and a recording of a demo like this one, so you can show it around."
- Priya sets a delivery time and keeps the tone warm: "[00:16:10] I'll have it all over to you by tomorrow morning."

**The near-miss.** Greg named the boss, Neil, and said Neil keeps asking about the corrugator. Greg also said there's budget this year if they move before December. The call ends with Greg saying "[00:16:33] Perfect. Yeah, I'll get back to you next week or so." We don't know who signs, and no meeting is booked. Priya could say:
- "Neil keeps asking why the corrugator goes down. Would he join a 30 minute look at that downtime report with you? Who else weighs in before you'd move ahead?"
- "You mentioned budget if you move before December. I have Thursday at 10 or Friday at 2. Which works for you and Neil?"

**Ask the room.**
1. Where in this call did Greg hand us the boss? What would you have asked at that point?
2. How would you ask for the boss without making Greg feel we're going around Greg?

### 3. Jordan, good: Ashgrove Plastics (Sep 22, first call)

**Why this step matters.** A meeting booked on the call, with the invite in the buyer's inbox before you hang up, is the difference between a next step and a hope. It also lets the buyer say what the next meeting has to show.

**Set it up.** Dana builds the business case at Ashgrove Plastics, a three-plant manufacturer. Luis runs the biggest plant and has to believe the techs will use a new system. The last one lasted about four months. Jordan has just played back the pain and Dana confirmed it.

**Play: [22:37 to 25:16](https://recorder.example/share/r7Kq2Ashgrove0922?t=1357).** Stop before Jordan asks what could stall it.

**What's good.**
- Proposes the next step in Dana's own terms, then asks for the calendar: "[00:22:37] Based on that, I think the right next step is a working session with you and Luis where we show the weekly schedule on a phone, the way a tech would use it on the floor. Can we get that on the calendar now? I have Wednesday the 30th at 10 Eastern or Thursday the 1st at 2."
- Sends the invite to both while on the call: "[00:23:42] Great. I'll send the invite now to you and Luis. Anyone else who should be there?"
- Confirms it landed: "[00:24:11] Sent. It should be in your inbox."
- Asks what the meeting has to prove: "[00:24:39] What would you and Luis need to see on Wednesday to know whether this is worth pursuing?"
- The answer becomes the plan for the session: "[00:24:52] Show Luis the weekly plan on a phone. If a tech can close a work order from the press in under a minute, he'll listen."

**The near-miss.** Dana said earlier that Karen Holt, the CFO, signs. The invite goes to Dana and Luis only. The next clip picks that up.

**Ask the room.**
1. Jordan asked "Anyone else who should be there?" What else could you ask to find the signer?
2. What does your buyer need to see in the next meeting to agree to the one after it?
