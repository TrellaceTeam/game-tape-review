## The clips to play

### 1. Priya, good: Harrow Creek (Oct 6, first call)

**Why this step matters.** The job in step 5 is to learn who signs and who else has a say, then get them onto the next call. A buyer who has to relay the pitch can only pass on what they remember. A buyer who hears it with the person who signs can say yes in the room.

**Set it up.** Ruth runs maintenance at Harrow Creek Castings, a two-plant foundry. A few minutes earlier she named the two people who decide. Walt Brenner is the plant manager and has to believe his crew will use it. Sandra Kim is the general manager and signs. Ruth said Sandra "won't really believe me, because I'm the one who wants it." Sandra also sets next year's budget the week before Thanksgiving.

**Play: [18:00 to 21:32](https://recorder.example/share/c2Hd6Harrow1006?t=1080).** Stop after Priya gives Ruth her cell number. The call ends a minute later.

**What's good.**
- Asks for both people and says why it's better for Ruth: "[00:18:04] Would Walt and Sandra be willing to join the next conversation? Honestly, I think it would go better than me trying to reach them through you."
- Shapes the meeting around what Sandra dislikes and Walt needs: "[00:18:29] Thirty minutes. And I'd want Walt doing most of the talking, not me." Ruth had said Sandra hates long vendor calls.
- Lets Walt's test set the agenda: "[00:18:43] If he can hand the phone to one of his own guys and watch him do it in under a minute, he'll get it. Can you set that up?"
- Books it on the call and frames it as good for the buyer: "[00:19:29] Then let's get it on the calendar while we're both here, so it doesn't turn into a week of email."
- Gives the reason Sandra should hear it from Walt: "[00:19:29] And the reason I'd push for it is so Sandra hears it from Walt, not from a vendor."
- Sends the invite while Ruth is still on: "[00:20:39] I'm sending it now to all three of you, Wednesday the 14th, 2 to 2:30 Eastern."
- Gives a direct line: "[00:21:09] And here's my cell, in case anything comes up before then."

**The near-miss.** Small. Ruth said Sandra might come ("Sandra, maybe, if it's short") and later that Sandra was "showing free." Only Ruth accepted on the call. A line like "Would you ask Sandra to hit accept today, so I know she's coming?" would close that gap.

**Ask the room.**
1. Ruth told Priya Sandra "won't really believe me." How did Priya use that in the ask?
2. Think of your last first call. Who in the deal would the buyer have trouble selling to, and what could you offer so they hear it from someone else?

### 2. Priya, pause: Sable Point (Oct 13, first call)

**Why this step matters.** Same step. When a buyer says "I'll need to run it by my boss," the buyer has just told you the person who signs, or the one who sways them, and has handed you the way to ask for them.

**Set it up.** Greg is the maintenance manager at Sable Point Packaging, a 250-person corrugated box plant. He's had three unplanned corrugator stops in August. Priya has just shown the product and quoted a price, and Greg took it well. We're about 15 minutes in.

**Play part 1: [15:05 to 15:50](https://recorder.example/share/q5Ws1Sable1013?t=905).** Pause right after Greg says "[00:15:43] I like it. I really do. I'll need to run it by my boss. He'll want to see numbers."

**Ask the room while it's paused.**
1. What would you say next?

**What came before.** The answer key, to share after the room answers.
- 9:31. Greg said, "[00:09:31] Sorry, one thing before you move on. I've got budget left this year if we move before December." Priya answered "Oh, that's great to know" and moved on.
- 12:02. Greg said, "[00:12:02] Does that break it out by machine? My plant manager, Neil Ostrowski, keeps asking me why the corrugator keeps going down." At 12:28 he added "[00:12:28] That'd be nice. Something to show him besides my spreadsheet. He's not a spreadsheet guy." The transcript doesn't say whether Neil is the boss from 15:43.
- 17:06. Greg said, "[00:17:06] We run hard until about the twentieth of December, and then we shut down for a week and do all the big jobs."
- The call has no question about who decides or who else weighs in. Greg offered each of these himself.

**Play part 2: [15:50 to 16:40](https://recorder.example/share/q5Ws1Sable1013?t=950).**

**What's good.**
- Credits the idea: "[00:15:54] Totally, that makes sense."
- Names the materials and a deadline: "[00:16:10] I'll send our overview deck, a one-pager on pricing, and a recording of a demo like this one, so you can show it around."
- Keeps it easy for Greg: "[00:16:40] Sounds great. No rush at all."

**The near-miss.** The call ends on "send some info," and the boss, who will judge the numbers, never hears them from us. Greg offered the chance twice: "run it by my boss" and "budget left this year if we move before December." Priya could have said:
- "Who's your boss, and would he join a 20 minute call so he sees numbers on your own corrugator? I have two times this week."
- "You said you have budget if you move before December. What does he need to see by when for that to happen?"

**Ask the room.**
1. Greg said the boss "will want to see numbers." What numbers could we bring to a call with the boss that Greg can't carry himself?
2. How would you ask to meet the boss without sounding like you're going around Greg?

### 3. Jordan, good: Ashgrove Plastics (Sept 22, first call)

**Why this step matters.** Same step. Saying the decision chain back to the buyer, and getting a yes, makes the next meeting an easy ask. The buyer has just agreed who needs to see what.

**Set it up.** Dana is VP of Operations at Ashgrove Plastics, a three-plant maker. Press 7 went down for 31 hours last quarter. Their biggest customer's supplier review is March 12th. Earlier Dana said she builds the case, Karen Holt (CFO) signs anything over $25,000, IT reviews it, and Luis Ortega, who runs the biggest plant, has to be on board or "it won't happen." Karen locks the budget in mid-November.

**Play: [21:15 to 24:39](https://recorder.example/share/r7Kq2Ashgrove0922?t=1275).** Stop after Dana says "Accepted."

**What's good.**
- Plays the decision chain back in the buyer's own facts: "[00:21:19] You build the case, Luis has to believe the techs will use it, IT reviews it, and Karen signs. Did I get that right?"
- Proposes a next step that fits the person who can stall the deal: "[00:22:37] I think the right next step is a working session with you and Luis where we show the weekly schedule on a phone, the way a tech would use it on the floor."
- Offers two times and books on the call: "[00:22:37] Can we get that on the calendar now? I have Wednesday the 30th at 10 Eastern or Thursday the 1st at 2."
- Sends the invite to both people while Dana is on: "[00:23:42] I'll send the invite now to you and Luis. Anyone else who should be there?"
- Dana confirms: "[00:24:26] Got it. Accepted. I'll forward a note to Luis so he knows what it is."

**The near-miss.** Karen signs, and she isn't on the invite. Dana answered "[00:23:58] Just Luis for this one. If he likes it, we'll bring IT in after." That was fair, because Luis is the real risk. A few minutes later, at 25:47, Jordan asked for a time on Karen's calendar and Dana said to wait ("Let's see how Wednesday goes first"). The next clip pair picks up what happened.

**Ask the room.**
1. Jordan asked "Anyone else who should be there?" What would you say to get the signer's name onto that question without overriding the buyer?
2. When the buyer says "just this person for now," what do you ask next so the signer still has a date?
