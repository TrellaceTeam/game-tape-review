## The clips to play

### 1. Jordan, good: Ashgrove Plastics (Sep 22, first call)

**Why this step matters.** The job is to learn who decides, what matters to each person and the steps to a yes. A deal moves when you know who signs and what that person needs to see.

**Set it up.** Dana runs operations at Ashgrove Plastics, a three-plant plastics maker. Earlier she said press 7 went down mid-run for their biggest customer and it cost about $55,000 in a quarter. A supplier review on March 12th is what makes this urgent. Jordan now asks how the company decides.

**Play: [15:50 to 18:35](https://recorder.example/share/r7Kq2Ashgrove0922?t=950).** Stop before Jordan gives a price range.

**What's good.**
- Asks an open question about how it works: "[00:15:57] Let me ask about how decisions get made. How do investments like this usually get made at Ashgrove?"
- Asks what the signer cares about: "[00:16:41] What will Karen care about most?"
- Finds the real risk, the plant lead: "[00:17:25] Luis, at our biggest plant. If he isn't on board, it won't happen. The other two plant managers will follow him."
- Reads the steps back: "[00:17:46] So if I've got the steps right, it's Luis seeing it and believing the techs will use it, then an IT review, then you take a case to Karen for sign-off."

**The near-miss.** Jordan learned who signs and what she cares about. He did not ask to meet her. He could have said:
- "Karen sounds like the person who decides. Could she join us for the working session, or a short call after it?"

**Ask the room.**
1. Think of your last first call. Who signed, and what did they care about? Could you have said it back like this?
2. Dana told us Karen wants payback inside a year. How would you ask to bring Karen in without going around Dana?
