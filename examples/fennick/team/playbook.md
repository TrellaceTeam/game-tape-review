# Fennick first-call playbook

Owner: Priya Raman. Last updated September 2026.

A first call has one goal: a second meeting on the calendar with the person who signs. Six steps, in this order.

## 1. Open (2 minutes)

- Thank them, confirm how long we have, and say what we'll cover.
- Required: "What made you take this call?"

## 2. Find the pain (10 minutes)

- Get one real example of a missed or late maintenance job, with the machine and the date.
- Ask what it cost: hours down, overtime, scrap, freight or an audit finding.
- Required: "What happens if this is still the same in six months?"

## 3. Play it back (1 minute)

- Say the pain back in their words, with their numbers, and get a yes.
- Required: "Did I get that right?"

## 4. Show the fix (8 minutes)

- Show only the screens that match their example. Start with the overdue list.
- When they start describing their own plant, stop and let them talk.

## 5. Map the decision (4 minutes)

- Learn who signs, who else has a say, and what each of them needs to see.
- Required: "Who else will want to weigh in before you'd move ahead?"
- Ask whether the person who signs can join the next call.

## 6. Book the next meeting (2 minutes)

- Book it before you hang up, with a date, a time and who's coming.
- Offer two times, and send the invite while you're still on the call.
- Don't end on "I'll send some info."

## Price, only when they ask

Give the range for their number of plants, and tie it to the cost they named. One plant runs $20,000 to $28,000 a year. No discounts on a first call.
